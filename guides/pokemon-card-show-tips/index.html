Pokémon card show tips: how to buy, sell and trade

A card show puts hundreds of binders in one room. A little prep before you walk in makes the difference between a good day and an expensive one.

River TCG · Updated November 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

Know what your cards and your want list are worth before you go, and keep checking sold prices on your phone at the table. Look at every card out of its sleeve before you pay. When you sell, expect vendors to offer below market price, with trade credit usually higher than cash, and get more than one offer.

Before you go

  • Make a want list with prices. Write down the cards you're after, the exact printing, and the most you'll pay. It keeps you from buying on impulse.
  • Sort what you're selling. Split your cards into bulk, mid-value and high-value, and know roughly what each high-value card sells for. Vendors move faster, and treat you more seriously, when your cards are organized.
  • Bring cash in small bills. Many vendors take cards or payment apps too, but cash is quick and sometimes gets a better price.
  • Charge your phone and bring a power bank. You'll be checking prices all day, and show halls often have bad signal.
  • Bring supplies: penny sleeves, toploaders or a small binder for what you buy, and a bag that closes.

Buying at a card show

Check the price yourself

Sticker prices are what the vendor is asking, not what the card is worth. Some are fair, some are well above market. Look up recent sold prices for the exact card, in the same condition or grade, before you agree to anything. For how to do that, see how much is my Pokémon card worth?

Inspect the card

Ask to take the card out of the sleeve or toploader, and look at it under good light. Check the corners, edges, the back and the surface for scratches. A card listed as Near Mint can turn out to be lightly played once it's out of the plastic. For graded cards, look up the cert number on the grader's website before you pay. See how to spot fake Pokémon cards for what to check.

Negotiate politely

  • Ask, don't demand. "What's your best price on this?" goes further than a lowball offer.
  • Bundle cards. Buying several cards from the same vendor usually gets a better total discount than haggling over one.
  • Mention cash if you're paying that way. Some vendors take less for cash because they skip card fees.
  • Shop late on the last day. Some vendors would rather sell than pack everything back up, so deals can be better near closing. The best cards may be gone by then, though.

Selling to vendors

Vendors buy cards to resell them, so they need room for a profit. That means an offer will be below market price. How far below depends on the card and the vendor:

  • Cards that sell fast, like popular chase cards in great condition, get the best offers.
  • Slow-moving cards and played copies get lower offers, and bulk is usually bought by the hundred or the thousand.
  • Trade credit is usually higher than cash. If you're going to spend the money at that table anyway, take the trade.

To get the best price:

  • Get offers from several vendors before you sell anything expensive. It's normal to say "thanks, let me look around".
  • Know your minimum for each card before you walk up to a table.
  • Ask how they're pricing it. Most vendors offer a percentage of a market price source. Ask which one, and check it yourself.

Trading

Trades are where the math gets fuzzy. A vendor might value your cards at their trade rate and their own cards at full sticker price. Before you agree:

  • Agree on a price source for both sides.
  • Add up what you're giving and what you're getting, at the same kind of price.
  • Check the condition of what you're getting as carefully as if you were paying cash.

If you're getting a table

Vending at a show is its own job. Price everything clearly before the doors open, keep track of what you paid for each card, and record every sale, buy and trade as it happens, so you know at the end of the weekend whether you actually made money after the table fee.

Etiquette and safety

  • Ask before you handle cards, and before taking photos of someone's display.
  • Don't block a table while you look things up. Step aside and come back.
  • Keep your binders and bags on you. Never leave them on a table or chair, even for a moment.
  • Wait for digital payments to arrive in your account before you hand over a card.
  • Count cash at the table, and keep large amounts out of sight.

Questions

How much do vendors pay for cards at a card show?

Less than market price, because they need room to resell. Popular cards in great condition get the best offers, while slow-moving cards, played copies and bulk get much less. Trade credit is usually higher than cash. Get offers from several vendors before you sell anything valuable.

Is it better to take cash or trade credit?

Trade credit is usually worth more than cash, so it's the better deal if you want cards from that same vendor. If you want money, or nothing at that table interests you, take cash.

When is the best time to find deals at a card show?

Late on the last day, when some vendors would rather sell than pack up. Early on the first day has the best selection, so if you want a specific card, go early.

Should I bring cash to a card show?

Yes. Many vendors take cards and payment apps, but cash is fast, works when signal is bad, and can get you a better price. Bring small bills.
